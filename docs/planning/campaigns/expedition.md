# Expedition campaign — working title: The Broken Frontier

Status: proposed campaign structure. Hybrid expedition/local command is user direction; names and story are proposals.

An expedition follows interrupted supply and distress signals through disconnected human settlements. The infestation adapts to each habitat. Local forces have useful knowledge and equipment, and their survival shapes later cooperation. Factions recur across places rather than being discarded after one chapter.

## Proposed story spine

1. Desert frontier: establish contact and recover a relay; meet mobile settlers.
2. Snow world: rescue a stranded survey force and learn that bugs reach supposedly sealed infrastructure.
3. Maritime world: protect evacuation and recover shipping access; command Navy defenders during one operation.
4. Industrial or jungle detour: choose a source of equipment or intelligence. Branching is a design option, not an engine requirement.
5. Capital world: local guards hold a district before expedition relief arrives; the crisis reaches the political center.
6. Orbital approach: recover evidence and assault the source of the coordinated spread. Final enemy identity remains open.

Other planet dossiers can support later chapters, optional operations or separate campaigns. Avoid committing every setting to the first release.

## Chapter rhythm

Arrival establishes a readable combat problem. Local-command contact introduces the regional force. Escalation combines a new threat with a familiar objective. A joint operation pays off local knowledge and expedition strength. Four missions are a useful writing pattern, not a fixed production quota.

## Progression proposal

Start implementation with standalone missions using existing selection/progress boundaries. Defer persistent soldiers, branching saves and cross-mission economies until separately refined. Narrative consequences can initially be briefings and recurring allies, with no unapproved mechanical rewards.

Difficulty must create pressure through attack routes, enemy replacement, resources and timing, with measured tests and player evidence. Increasing counts alone does not establish difficulty. Every mission needs a loss condition, victory gate, reinforcement rules and cap behavior.
